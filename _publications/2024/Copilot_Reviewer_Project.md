abstract: >-
  This study examines how AI-based code reviewers reshape contribution behavior and maintenance dynamics in open-source software (OSS) communities. AI reviewers can provide scalable, fast, and standardized evaluations. They can potentially reshape contributions as well as maintainers' workload. Leveraging the introduction of GitHub Copilot Reviewer on GitHub as a natural experiment in 2025, we analyze 974,654 pull requests (PRs) between 2024 and 2026. Using maintainer-level panel data in a staggered difference-in-differences setup, we investigate how AI review affects maintainers' workload and review efficiency as well as contribution behavior. We find that repository maintainers initially face a sharp increase in PR submissions, both by humans and bots, together with a higher review workload after the adoption of the AI reviewer. Over time, however, human contributions decline while the increase in bot-generated PRs is sustained. Interestingly, survival analysis reveals that AI reviewers increase review efficiency for human PR contributions, as they are merged faster, whereas bot PRs take longer to review and merge. Our findings suggest that while AI reviewers increase review efficiency, they aggravate the maintenance burden and maintainers' workload by encouraging more code contributions via humans and bots.
